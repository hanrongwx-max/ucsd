# UC San Diego Lab Safety — Organized Summary and Analysis

Source: UC San Diego Blink EH&S pages (https://blink.ucsd.edu/safety/ehs/index.html and the linked Research Safety pages), retrieved 2026-10-23. Each point links back to the page notes under `../en/`.

## 1. How the system is built

UC San Diego lab safety rests on three layers:

| Layer | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulation** | Cal/OSHA Laboratory Standard (8 CCR §5191), HazCom (§5194), IIPP (§3203), PPE (§3380), BBP (§5193), ATD (§5199); federal OSHA, NIH Guidelines, BMBL, DEA; County CUPA / HMBP; California Fire Code | [chemical-hygiene-plan](../en/02-chemical/chemical-hygiene-plan.md), [bsl-practices-chart](../en/01-biosafety/bsl-practices-chart.md) |
| **Institutional policy** | UC systemwide policies (PPE, training, minors), UCSD PPM 516-3 / 516-5 / 516-7 / 516-14 / 516-19, Chemical Hygiene Plan, Laboratory Safety Manual, Biosafety Program, Radiation Safety Manual | [uc-lab-safety-policies](../en/08-training-policy/uc-lab-safety-policies.md), [laboratory-safety-manual](../en/05-laboratory-safety-manual/laboratory-safety-manual.md) |
| **Lab-level controls** | Authorizations (BUA, CSUA, RUA, LUA), Hazard Control Plans (= SOPs), LHAT (PPE assessment), training records, inventories — all managed in the **My Research Safety** portal | [hazard-control-plan](../en/02-chemical/hazard-control-plan.md), [biohazard-use-authorization](../en/01-biosafety/biohazard-use-authorization.md) |

**Key insight:** at UCSD the *Hazard Control Plan is the SOP*. Nothing hazardous starts until (1) an HCP exists, (2) the PI approves it, and (3) each worker acknowledges it. The same "approve before work" gate applies to BUAs (IBC), CSUAs (DEA/CSP), RUAs and LUAs.

## 2. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Core duties |
|---|---|
| **Principal Investigator (PI)** | Single point of accountability. Obtains authorizations; adopts and approves HCPs; completes LHAT and selects PPE; ensures and documents training (keep ≥ 3 years); provides exposure control plans; addresses audit findings; New PI Orientation before operating a lab. |
| **Lab worker** | Complete training before work; follow SOPs/HCPs; understand hazards and PPE limits; get approval before restricted materials; report accidents and unsafe conditions immediately; join medical surveillance if required. ([13 duties](../en/05-laboratory-safety-manual/laboratory-safety-manual.md)) |
| **Area Safety Coordinator (ASC)** | Monitors first-aid kits, Emergency Guides, local compliance; requests extra PPE. |
| **EH&S (RAP, Biosafety, Chemical Hygiene Officer, Radiation, Fire & Life Safety, EMF)** | Audits (twice yearly for most labs), consultations, waste collection, certifications (fume hoods annually, eyewash monthly), incident review. |
| **Committees** | IBC (biohazards), CSSC (chemicals), RSC (radiation), IACUC (animals). |

## 3. Top rules every lab member must know (the "non-negotiables")

1. **Never work alone** with hazardous chemicals.
2. **Minimum attire at all times:** full-length pants + closed-toe, closed-heel shoes; personally fitted lab coat and safety glasses (shared coats are not compliant).
3. **No eating, drinking, cosmetics, or contact-lens handling** except in an EH&S-approved, posted Clean Area (≥ 1 m or physical barrier); never in BSL-2+/BSL-3.
4. **No mouth pipetting; never smell chemicals; never recap needles.**
5. **Read the SDS before using any chemical**; treat unknowns as highly toxic; mixtures as more hazardous than the worst component.
6. **Label every container** (name, concentration, hazard; dates for peroxide formers and reactives); keep containers closed.
7. **Store by compatibility group, not alphabetically**; flammables never in household refrigerators.
8. **Secure every gas cylinder** with upper and lower non-combustible restraints — no C-clamps.
9. **No hazardous waste down the drain, by evaporation, or in the trash** — illegal, with personal liability.
10. **Report every injury, exposure, and near miss immediately.**

## 4. Hazard domains — key numbers

### 4.1 Chemical
- Building ventilation ≥ 6 ACH; fume hood face velocity 100 fpm, certified annually; ductless hoods prohibited; hoods are not for biohazards.
- Vacuum-trap filters replaced at least every 90 days; dry-ice/isopropanol cold traps (−78 °C); liquid-nitrogen traps only on sealed/evacuated systems.
- Inventory: six-month supply target; MAQ limits per control area (e.g., 120 gal flammable liquids sample), +100% for full sprinklers, +100% for approved cabinets; pyrophorics only in fully sprinklered buildings.
- Environmental (cold/warm) rooms: no storage of flammables; ≤ 500 mL in use.
- Corrosive gas cylinders ≤ 6 months; oxidizer gases ≥ 20 ft from flammables.
- High-hazard list (HF, perchloric acid, picric acid, pyrophorics, piranha, sodium azide, EtBr, cryogens, etc.) requires a chemical-specific HCP. Dry picric acid is explosive.

### 4.2 Biological
- Four containment levels (BSL-1, 2, 2+, 3); no Risk Group 4 agents at UCSD.
- IBC approval before any biohazardous or r/sNA work; BUA renewals every 1/3/5 years; 60 days to answer "approve pending".
- Class II A2 BSC, certified annually and after every move; one user at a time; no open flames.
- Liquid biohazardous waste: bleach 1:9 for 30 minutes; red-bag waste must reach EMF within 7 calendar days.
- Bloodborne pathogens: training before work and annually; free Hep B vaccine; PEP works best when started early.
- Biological spill: wait 30 min for aerosols, 20 min disinfectant contact time.

### 4.3 Hazardous waste
- WGN required before collection; tag before first drop of waste; ownership, start date, contents, hazards.
- 90 days (chemical, mixed) / 240 days (universal, radioactive, non-hazardous, recyclable); 55 gal → collect within 3 days.
- Secondary containment ≥ 110 %; ~10 % headspace; screw caps only; no solvents mixed with toxic metals.
- Drain rules: pH 5–12.5 only, < 24 % ethanol, < 2.9 % formaldehyde, < 8 % H₂O₂, never EtBr; dilution to meet limits is prohibited.
- Sharps: five categories; fill line; biohazard sharps to collection within 7 days of closing.

### 4.4 Physical / equipment
- Centrifuge: HCP required; rotor logbooks; balance; wait 15 min after a break (30 min for biohazards).
- Fire: ABC extinguisher within 75 ft, ≥ 4A:60B:C, monthly checks; 18 in sprinkler clearance; PASS technique.
- Eyewash/shower: reachable in 10 s (~55 ft), flush ≥ 15 min, inspected monthly.
- Slips/trips: keep floors dry and aisles clear (29 CFR 1910.22).

### 4.5 Special populations
- Minors: < 14 not allowed (except tours); 14–18 with consent and constant supervision; never in controlled-substance labs.
- Reproductive hazards: confidential voluntary declaration; Reproductive Hazard HCP.
- Nanomaterials: assume toxic; double nitrile gloves; HEPA capture; wet wipe; never sweep.

### 4.6 Controlled substances and radiation
- CSUA before use; secure storage; usage logs; training every 4 years; DEA inspects unannounced.
- RUA for radioactive materials; all deliveries via Radiation Safety; monitor and record cpm after spills.

## 6. Emergency response — one-page logic

1. **Life first:** remove from exposure → flush 15 min (eyes/skin) → call 911 / Poison Control (800) 222-1222.
2. **Assess:** small and trained? clean up with PPE from outside edge inward. Large, unknown, or dangerous? evacuate, close doors, call EH&S (858) 534-3660 (after hours 911 / (858) 534-4357).
3. **Report:** supervisor immediately; online incident report; serious injuries reach Cal/OSHA within 8 hours.
4. **Preserve:** for serious incidents secure the scene and evidence for ≥ 24 hours.

## 7. Inspection exposure (what auditors actually find)

Most common CUPA finding: **ethidium bromide waste** (untagged gel buckets, open buffer containers). Other frequent issues: overfilled sharps containers, unsecured cylinders, missing training records, incompatible storage, blocked exits/sprinklers, extension cords. Use the Lab Self-Audit Form before RAP visits.

## 8. Takeaways for teaching

- Teach the **approval chain** (authorization → HCP → training → acknowledgement → work) as the backbone.
- Teach **numbers** students must memorise (15 min, 10 s, 90 days, 7 days, 110 %, 1:9 bleach / 30 min, 18 in, 75 ft).
- Teach **"stop and ask"** decision points: unknown chemical, spill size, needlestick, dry picric acid, alarm on a hood.
- Use **real UCSD lessons**: 2009 vacuum-pump explosion, sprinkler obstruction flood, freezer-ice slip.

These points feed the courseware, question bank, and video scripts in `../training/`.

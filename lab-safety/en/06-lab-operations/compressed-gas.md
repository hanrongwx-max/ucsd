# Compressed Gas: Storage & Handling

Researchers must follow an approved, PI-preapproved Hazard Control Plan for compressed gases; this page supplements it.

## Storage
- Cool, dry, well-ventilated area. **Never store cylinders in cold or warm rooms** (except compressed air).
- Not next to doors or in corridors.
- Oxidizers (e.g., oxygen) at least **20 feet** from flammables or separated by a non-combustible wall.
- Label cylinders full / in-use / empty; rotate inventory; store full and empty separately (empty cylinders on pressurized systems can cause suck-back).
- Review inventory monthly. Do not keep non-corrosive gases more than 10 years past the hydrostatic test date. **Never store corrosive gases longer than 6 months** (ammonia, HCl, chlorine, methylamine). Return damaged/corroded cylinders.

## Restraints
- Secure all cylinders at all times, empty or full, upright, with **upper and lower non-combustible restraints** (chain and Unistrut preferred), tight.
- Upper restraint at least 1 ft below the shoulder region; lower restraint at least 1 ft above the floor.
- **C-clamps and bench brackets are not allowed.** At most 3 cylinders per restraint. Cap cylinders when not in use.
- (1994 Northridge earthquake: double-chained cylinders held; C-clamps failed.)

## Operations
- Read the SDS; label cylinder and gas line with the gas name (don't rely on color).
- Use the correct dedicated regulator — **never adapters**.
- Keep flames and sparks away; flashback protectors on flammable gas units.
- Use a trap or check valve when discharging gas into a liquid.

## Leaks
- **Hazardous gas:** leave, close the door, secure the room, sound the fire alarm, call 9-1-1 (cell (858) 534-4357; Medical Center 6-1-1-1 / (619) 543-6222) and give the gas name.
- **Non-hazardous gas:** close the valve; if still leaking, cap and call EH&S (858) 534-3660; if stopped, call STORE (858) 534-7467.

## Transport
Keep the valve cap on; use a hand truck with chain/strap even for short distances; never roll, drag, slide, or lift by the cap. Return empties with positive pressure, cap on, labeled "empty."

## Flammable, Oxidizing, Corrosive Gases
- **Flammable:** 20 ft from oxidizers or 18-inch-extended non-combustible partition; away from ignition, panels, corridors; minimize quantities; consider diluted mixtures; flow restrictors; possible detection systems. N2/H2 above 5% H2 and Ar/H2 above 3% H2 are flammable.
- **Hydrogen:** stainless steel tubing; continuous leak watch; ventilation; purge below 1% before maintenance. Anaerobic chambers need O2 and H2 sensors.
- **Oxidizing:** keep oil and grease away (explosion risk); clean regulators/tubing; oxygen piping must be steel, brass, copper, or stainless and installed by Facilities.
- **Corrosive:** inspect valves for corrosion; use caution if flow doesn't start (possible plug).

## Toxic / High-Hazard Gases (Classes I–III)
Use the classification chart and requirements matrix; handle in a fume hood; use small cylinders. **Avoid lecture bottles** (interchangeable fittings risk mixing incompatibles).

## Helium Balloons
Same cylinder rules; ventilated area; close the valve after use; never leave unattended; **never let anyone breathe helium**.

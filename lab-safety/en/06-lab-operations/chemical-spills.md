# How to Handle Chemical Spills in Laboratories

Core rule: avoid working alone when hazardous chemicals are involved.

## 1. Plan and Prepare
- Post the UCSD Emergency Guide flipchart near lab phones; make sure personnel read the spill procedures. Write SOPs for chemicals the Guide does not cover.
- Assemble a spill kit for small spills of your common chemicals; keep it stocked and reachable; train staff on its contents and when cleanup is safe.
- Everyone must know where fire extinguishers, pull stations, eye washes, showers, and phones are; how and when to use an extinguisher; how to use the eye wash and shower.

## 2. Evaluate the Spill
- **Large or extremely dangerous:** immediate hazard (fire, explosion, exposure); any spill of a highly dangerous chemical; moderate or large spills. If unsure, treat as large → Step 3.
- **Small, incidental:** can be cleaned up without endangering anyone → Step 4.

## 3. Get Help for Large or Dangerous Spills
- EH&S Hazardous Materials Response: business hours **(858) 534-3660**; after hours Campus Police **(858) 534-4357 (534-HELP)**.
- Avoid breathing vapors; identify the material if safe; turn off ignition sources for flammables if safe; alert people, evacuate, close doors.
- If someone is splashed: flush **at least 15 minutes**, call Poison Control (800) 222-1222, seek medical attention.
- Keep people away; lock doors; post warning signs; have someone knowledgeable brief responders.

## 4. Clean Up Small Spills Safely
1. Alert people, avoid vapors, identify the material.
2. Flush any splashed person for 15 minutes; call Poison Control.
3. Wear PPE: safety goggles, gloves, long-sleeved lab coat.
4. Confine the spill and absorb it; put saturated absorbent in a plastic bag with a hazardous waste tag for the next collection.
5. Clean the area with water.
6. Restock the spill kit.

# Bloodborne Pathogens Program

UC San Diego's Bloodborne Pathogens (BBP) Program protects employees who can reasonably expect to be exposed to bloodborne pathogens in their job duties.

## Exposure Control Plan Requirement

If an employee could possibly be exposed to bloodborne pathogens at work, the principal investigator or supervisor must put a written Exposure Control Plan in place. Requirements differ for laboratory personnel, non-laboratory personnel (such as emergency responders and sports trainers), and healthcare personnel.

## Laboratory Personnel

PIs with employees who may be exposed to BBPs must:
- Complete the Bloodborne Pathogen Hazard Control Plan (BBP HCP).
- Follow the Engineered Sharps Protection Requirements for Bloodborne Pathogens.
- Make sure personnel complete:
  - **Biosafety: Bloodborne Pathogens** training before starting work and annually after that.
  - **Lab-specific training** covering hazards associated with the research, personal protective equipment, and safety practices and behaviors.

## Non-Laboratory Personnel

Supervisors must complete an Exposure Control Plan, ensure employees complete Biosafety: Bloodborne Pathogens training before beginning work and annually, and may contact EH&S Biosafety for customized group training.

## Healthcare Personnel

UC San Diego Medical Center facilities have location-specific Exposure Control Plans and training. Medical Center Safety Office: (619) 543-7575.

## Hepatitis B Vaccination

- Strongly recommended for employees with occupational exposure to BBPs; provided by COEM at no cost.
- Offered during initial BBP training via (1) UC Learning Center "Bloodborne Pathogen Training and Hepatitis B Vaccination Decision" course, or (2) the Acceptance/Declination form returned to EH&S.
- After the 3-dose series, a hepatitis B surface antibody titer test is strongly recommended (also free).
- Declining employees may accept the vaccine later while still exposed. Contact ehsbio@ucsd.edu to update a decision.

## Exposure Procedures

- Follow the UCSD Emergency Guide's Needlestick/Exposure to Blood & Body Fluids protocol.
- **Report all exposure incidents immediately.**

### Post-Exposure Evaluation and Follow-Up

A confidential medical evaluation and follow-up are available immediately. Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) works best when started quickly. **Clean the wound, then seek medical attention right away.**
- Business hours: call COEM and ask for an emergency consultation with an Occupational Medicine Nurse — COEM Hillcrest (619) 471-9210; COEM La Jolla (858) 657-1600.
- After hours: go directly to the emergency room at UC San Diego Thornton Hospital or Hillcrest Medical Center.

## Universal Precautions and Definitions

Universal precautions treat all human blood and certain body fluids as potentially infectious.

**Bloodborne pathogens** include HBV, HCV, and HIV (among others).

**Other potentially infectious materials (OPIM):** semen and vaginal secretions; cerebrospinal, synovial, pleural, pericardial, peritoneal, and amniotic fluids; saliva in dental procedures; any body fluid visibly contaminated with blood; all undifferentiated body fluids in emergencies; unfixed human tissue or organs; human cell, tissue, or organ cultures; experimental animals with human xenografts.

**Generally not covered** unless they contain visible blood: feces, nasal secretions, sputum, sweat, tears, urine, vomit, and saliva.

# Biosafety Program

Learn about the UC San Diego institutional Biosafety Program. University policy requires that all research, clinical, and teaching activities involving biohazardous materials be conducted safely to protect the campus and the wider community.

## About the Biosafety Program

- **Purpose:** The program, adopted by the Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC), sets practices, procedures, facility construction, and operational standards for handling biohazardous materials.
- **Scope:** Applies to faculty, staff, hosted visitors, students, participating guests and volunteers, contract laborers, supplemental personnel, and employees of firms working where the University manages specific biohazards. It does not apply to UC San Diego Medical Center hospitals or clinics covered under the University Medical Center license.
- **Implementation:** Implemented by the Biosafety Officer in Environment, Health & Safety (EH&S).

## Biohazardous Materials

- **Definition:** Infectious agents or hazardous biological materials that pose a risk, or potential risk, to human, animal, or environmental health, through direct infection or indirect environmental damage.
- **Restriction:** "NO Risk Group 4 Agents may be used or stored at UC San Diego."
- **Covered materials include:**
  - Infectious organisms and agents that can cause disease in humans, animals, or plants, or cause significant environmental or agricultural impact
  - Infectious prions
  - Viral vectors
  - Engineered cell-entry domains (e.g., TAT fusion proteins)
  - Nanomaterials used with biological systems, agents, or materials
  - Human and non-human primate tissues, fluids, cells, or cell cultures
  - Animals, tissues, fluids, cells, or cell cultures exposed to infectious organisms or known zoonotic reservoirs
  - Recombinant or synthetic nucleic acids in vitro, in vivo, in clinical trials, or with a chance of environmental release
  - Transgenic plants or animals
  - Human gene transfer clinical trials
  - Biological toxins
  - Select agents
- Related: High-Risk Biological Agents: Medical Consultation Requirement

## Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC)

The IBC oversees biohazard use by:
- Creating and enforcing policies and procedures
- Reviewing biohazard use
- Monitoring the construction and use of containment facilities

The IBC reviews and approves all projects involving biohazardous materials before work begins. All BUA new submissions, renewals, and amendments must be submitted through the BUA application by the deadline to be reviewed at the corresponding meeting. Deadlines are strictly enforced because of the extensive pre-review process. Questions: ehsbio@ucsd.edu.

## Signs and Labels

- **Biohazard signs:** Use the biohazard symbol (black on orange is common). Larger equipment that holds or uses biohazardous materials, such as BSCs, incubators, refrigerators, and freezers, must be visibly labeled.
- **Biosafety level signs:** Posted at facility entrances to indicate the BSL. Facilities are BSL 1, 2, or 3, with 3 the highest. Unauthorized entry into BSL 3 labs is prohibited.
- **Clean area signs:** Eating, drinking, food storage, cosmetics application, and contact lens handling are permitted only in approved, posted clean areas. Clean areas are not allowed where aerosol transmissible pathogens are manipulated or in rooms approved at BSL 2+ or 3.

## Storage

- Biohazardous materials must be clearly identified and stored to prevent accidental exposure.
- This normally means double containment and labeling freezers and refrigerators with the biohazard symbol.

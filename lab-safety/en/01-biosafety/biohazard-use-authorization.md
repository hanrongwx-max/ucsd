# Apply for and Manage a Biohazard Use Authorization (BUA)

## Introduction

- Access the BUA through the My Research Safety web portal (https://ehs.ucsd.edu/myresearchsafety/do/mrsHome).
- Principal investigators (PIs) must get approval from the UC San Diego Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC) before starting research, clinical, or teaching work with biohazardous materials or recombinant or synthetic nucleic acids.
- Two committees use the online BUA application: the **Campus IBC** and the **Human Gene Therapy IBC (HGT IBC)**.
- An approved BUA will: describe the work with biohazardous materials; set the biosafety level of containment; identify researchers, lab workers, and collaborators; authorize the PI to conduct the work; identify approved work locations.
- PIs must start a BUA, keep it current, and submit it for IBC review. PI Contacts named in the BUA can view it, add personnel, and edit drafts.
- The campus IBC meets virtually on the second Friday of each month, 9–11 a.m. The HGT IBC meets on the second Tuesday of each month.

## Overview

### IBC Review

- The IBC reviews all proposed biohazardous work in a laboratory or clinical setting, as a whole, regardless of funding source (including unfunded work).
- The PI is the single point of accountability and responsibility for a laboratory or clinical trial.
- **Campus IBC** reviews work with biohazardous materials, human source materials, environmental samples, and recombinant or synthetic nucleic acids, including unfunded, federally funded, PI-funded, and FDA-approved compassionate use work. Work cannot begin until the IBC approves the protocol.
- **Human Gene Therapy IBC** reviews administration of recombinant or synthetic nucleic acid molecules (NIH Guidelines III-C-1) to human research participants. It is administered by Sabai Global for a fee (clientservices@sabaiglobal.com). Externally administered IBCs are not permitted for UC San Diego research.
- A PI is the person responsible for the project and everyone involved, who is assigned lab space or conducts a human gene transfer clinical trial.

### BUA Application

IBC approval is required before starting work involving items such as:
- New infectious agents, viruses, or vector systems
- Enhanced replication or infectivity of viruses or microorganisms
- Biological toxins
- Human or primate cell lines
- Nucleic acids
- Changes in genetic constructs or model systems
- Procedures with increased aerosolization or other exposure risk
- Any recombinant nucleic acids used in vitro, in vivo, or in clinical trials

(The list is not exhaustive.)

### Submission Dates

- BUAs are reviewed at the IBC's monthly meeting. Deadlines are strictly enforced.
- Exceptions may be made for emergency use or expanded access involving patients — email EHSBio@ucsd.edu and request expedited review.

## Life Cycle of a BUA

1. **Initiation** — PI starts New BUA / Renew / Amend from My BUAs. Saved as Draft, editable by PI and PI Contacts. A new BUA receives a BUA number; amendments and renewals keep the same number.
2. **Complete Content and Submit** — complete all required sections and applicable Hazard Control Plans; identify personnel training and roles; describe materials and containment practices; PI certifies accuracy. Application locks upon submission.
3. **Administrative Review** — completeness, formatting, documentation, personnel eligibility, training records. Either *Return to PI* (PI Revise) or *Advance to BioStaff Review*.
4. **BioStaff Review** — biosafety professionals check technical accuracy, perform a risk assessment, may conduct a site visit; evaluate containment, practices, equipment, facilities, decontamination, waste disposal, emergency procedures. Some low-risk research may use a designated review process (not for NIH Guidelines III-A to III-E).
5. **IBC Review** — decisions: Approved; Acknowledge Approval of Designated Reviewed BUAs; Approve Pending; Defer; Not approved. For Approve Pending or Deferred, the lab has **60 days** to respond, otherwise the protocol is Not Approved.
6. **Compliance and Approval** — status becomes Approved; PI receives the IBC Approval Letter listing approval conditions. **Approval conditions must be met before work starts.**

## How to Apply for a BUA

**First-time applicants** must: (1) be entered in the EH&S database via the PI Information form; (2) have a UCSD Active Directory / Business Systems (SSO) username and password.

**Apply:** My Research Safety → Authorizations tab → open the application → follow the welcome screen.

- Type 1: Research BUA
- Type 2: Clinical Trial BUA (human gene transfer)

**PI Contacts** (Section 9): editing privileges (edit drafts, add/remove personnel, view, receive emails) or view-only privileges.

## Amend Your BUA

The PI must notify EH&S and get IBC approval through an amendment before changing any variable that may increase biohazard risk. Amendments are required for:
- Adding or deleting locations
- Adding or deleting biological materials
- Changes in genetic constructs
- Changes in SOPs
- Changes in participant enrollment (clinical trial BUA)
- Updates to Serious Adverse Events (clinical trial BUA)

**Amendments require IBC approval before new work begins.** The amendment project summary should include project description, removed materials, planned procedures and assays, and risks and mitigation measures.

## Renew a BUA

- BUAs must be renewed every **1, 3, or 5 years**, depending on risk.
- A renewal notice is emailed starting **2 months** before the renewal date.
- Early submission (more than 2 months early) may extend the next cycle by up to **6 months**.
- Previously approved research may continue during renewal; new work cannot start until IBC approval. If submitted after the renewal date, research cannot occur until an active approval is issued.
- The project summary must be rewritten for every renewal, even if nothing changed.

## Update Personnel or Training Anytime

Without an amendment, a PI or PI Contact can add/remove authorized personnel (who must first appear on the My EH&S Profile lab roster), add/remove PI Contacts, and assign required biosafety training (completed through UC Learning Center).

## BUA Verification Letter

For funding agencies that require specific biosafety assurance language, complete the BUA Verification Letter request form; the Biosafety Officer signs it. Put "URGENT" in the subject line for urgent requests.

## Deactivate a BUA

A BUA can be closed only if: all activity has stopped; all biological materials have been removed and disposed of; all work surfaces and equipment have been decontaminated. Notify EH&S Biosafety by email with the BUA number, PI name, and how materials were removed. Clinical trial BUAs: one month since last dosing, investigational drugs destroyed or returned, study closed to enrollment.

## Transfer a BUA

The receiving PI creates a new BUA (noting "BUA transfer", previous BUA number and PI) or amends an existing one, and is added as personnel on the original BUA to copy information.

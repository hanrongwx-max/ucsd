# Biological Safety Cabinets (BSC): Overview

Use a BSC ("biosafety cabinet," "tissue culture hood") for procedures that can create airborne biohazards. BSCs protect workers, the environment, and research materials. Label BSCs, incubators, refrigerators, freezers with the biohazard symbol.

**Not equivalent:** chemical fume hoods (no HEPA filters — not for biohazards); clean benches (protect the product only — never for biohazardous, toxic, or sensitizing material).

## Certification
- **Annual certification** required; uncertified or malfunctioning BSCs must not be used and are tagged "Out of Order."
- **Re-certify after any move**, even small ones (HEPA filter can be disturbed).

## Placement (minimum clearances)
6 in from walls/columns, between BSCs, and on sides/behind; 40 in open space in front; 60 in from opposing walls/benches/traffic; 20 in from perpendicular bench tops; 100 in between facing BSCs; 60 in from behind a doorway; 40 in from a door's swing side; 6 in from its hinge side. Consider seismic restraints.

## Relocation or Decommissioning
A certified professional must **gas-decontaminate** the BSC before relocation or decommissioning.

## Open Flames
**Open flames and flammable gases are prohibited in BSCs** at UC San Diego (see policy).

## Recommended Type
**Class II, Type A2** — protects personnel, environment, and product; inflow ≥ 75 lfpm; HEPA-filtered supply and exhaust. Class I (no product protection) and Class III (glove box) are rarely used.

## Usage
Hands-on training available (ehsbio@ucsd.edu). **One user at a time** — multiple users disrupt airflow.

Regulations: CDC BMBL; PPM 516-3; PPM 516-19

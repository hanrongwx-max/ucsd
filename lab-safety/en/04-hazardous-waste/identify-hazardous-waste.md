# How to Identify Hazardous Chemical Waste

Individual generators are responsible for making their own hazardous waste determination. If the material has a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) and you would be reluctant to eat, drink, or wear it, it is probably hazardous waste under California regulations. Unknowns or questions: hazwaste@ucsd.edu, (858) 534-2753.

## 1. Is the chemical a "waste"?

Treat as waste any material that is: spent; unusable (out of specification); past its expiration date; **unlabeled for more than 10 days**; abandoned; a container that once held chemicals; unwanted and intended for disposal or recycling.

## 2. Is it "extremely hazardous" waste?

Check the List of Hazardous and Extremely Hazardous Wastes. Otherwise, it is extremely hazardous if **any** of these is YES:
- Acute oral LD50 ≤ 50 mg/kg, acute dermal LD50 ≤ 43 mg/kg, or acute inhalation LC50 ≤ 100 ppm (gas/vapor)
- Human exposure likely to cause death, disabling injury, or serious illness (carcinogenicity, high toxicity, bioaccumulation, persistence)
- Water reactive

## 3. Is it "hazardous" waste?

YES to any of the following means hazardous waste:
- **Solid reagent chemicals in a manufacturer's container?**
- **Ignitable?** flashpoint ≤ 140 °F (60 °C); can cause fire through friction, moisture, or spontaneous change; ignitable compressed gas; oxidizer; releases oxygen; ignites organic materials.
- **Corrosive?** liquid pH ≤ 2 or ≥ 12.5; solid with that pH when mixed with equal weight of water; corrodes steel; destroys living tissue.
- **Reactive?** unstable; reacts violently with water; cyanide/sulfide-bearing waste releasing toxic gas at pH 2–12.5; can detonate or explode.
- **Toxic?** oral LD50 < 2,500 mg/kg; dermal LD50 < 4,300 mg/kg; inhalation LC50 < 10,000 ppm; aquatic 96-h LC50 < 500 mg/L; carcinogen/mutagen/teratogen, chronic toxicity, bioaccumulation, persistence.

If NO to all: solids may go in regular trash (do not dissolve powders or salts to pour down a drain); liquids may go down a drain, possibly after neutralizing (see sewer guidance).

**Note:** Many hazardous wastes remain hazardous at concentrations below 1%.

## 4. Determination Assistance

**Laboratory wastes are presumed hazardous unless proven, beyond a reasonable doubt, to be nonhazardous.** If still unsure, complete the Waste Determination Form with SDSs, process knowledge, and SOPs. Contacts: dlapel@ucsd.edu, (858) 583-3285; hazwaste@ucsd.edu, (858) 534-2753.

# How to Store and Dispose of Hazardous Chemical Waste

Covers hazardous chemical waste from container selection through EH&S collection. Radioactive and biohazardous waste have separate procedures. Extremely hazardous wastes follow separate procedures.

## Designate a Hazardous Waste Storage Area

- Near where waste is generated, under lab personnel control, out of the way of normal activities.
- Post a "Danger – Hazardous Waste" sign (EH&S provides on request).
- Fume hoods may temporarily hold small quantities, but are **not** designated storage areas.

## Select Compatible Containers

- Container and lid must be chemically compatible. **Acids and bases: not in metal. Hydrofluoric acid: not in glass. Gasoline/solvents: not in lightweight polyethylene (milk jugs).**
- Use **leak-proof, screw-on caps**; corks, parafilm, and beakers are not acceptable.
- Keep containers closed except when adding waste; wipe them down before collection.
- Match container size to quantity.
- **Secondary containment** for every waste container: chemically compatible, holding **at least 110%** of the primary container volume. EH&S provides free secondary containers for 20-L (5-gal) waste containers.

## Tag Every Waste Container

Attach a completed hazardous waste tag **before** adding waste. Cross out other labels, but do not remove the original product label.

## Liquid Waste

- Do not overfill — leave about **10% headspace**.
- Do not mix solids with liquids.
- Small liquid-filled containers (vials, Eppendorf tubes): double-bag in clear bags, same constituents only, seal each bag, list contents.
- Halogenated and non-halogenated solvents may be combined; contact EH&S before adding other constituents. **Do not combine organic solvents with toxic metal waste** (As, Ba, Cd, Cr, Pb, Hg, Se, Ag, Cu, Ni, Zn).
- Waste petroleum oils are recycled — keep them separate from contaminated oils.

## Dry, Solid Waste

- **Lab trash** (absorbent paper, Kimwipes, gloves, benchcoat): double-bag in clear plastic bags; EH&S cannot collect bags whose contents cannot be seen.
- **Dry chemicals:** dispose of in the manufacturer's container with a tag.
- **Sharps** (pipettes, tips, broken glass): special packaging — see sharps guidance.

## Unknown Chemical Waste

Unknowns are treated as hazardous and are costly. Avoid them by labeling and dating all inventory. If found: ask others; if identified, tag it; if not, notify your Department Safety Officer and RAP Specialist, complete the Unknown Chemical/Substance Hazard Assessment Checklist, then tag and request collection.

## Storage Time and Quantity Limits

- **Time:** collected within **90 days** of when waste was first placed in the container.
- **Quantity:** up to **55 gallons** of any individual hazardous waste; once 55 gallons accumulate, collection is required **within three days**.

## Prohibited Disposal

Disposing of hazardous waste down sinks, by intentional evaporation, or in regular trash is against the law.

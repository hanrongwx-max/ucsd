# Nanotechnology: Guidelines for Safe Research Practices

Nanotechnology concerns matter at roughly 1–100 nm. Toxicity data is limited — "it is prudent to assume the nanoparticles may be toxic."

## 1. Safety Training (PI must provide documented training)
- Written safety protocol in the research procedure; read SDSs, manufacturer instructions, SOPs (SDS data may reflect the bulk material).
- Four exposure routes: **inhalation** (deep lung penetration), **ingestion**, **injection** (needles, animals), **skin absorption**.
- Train on PPE, engineering controls, emergency procedures; reactive nanoparticle workers need fire extinguisher training.
- Records: topics, materials, date, names, signatures.

## 2. Hazard Assessment
Consult SDSs, NIOSH "Approaches to Safe Nanotechnology," UC guidelines, and the Nanotoolkit (PDF: https://blink.ucsd.edu/_files/safety-tab/research/laboratory/nanoToolKit2011.pdf). Consider precursor hazards. **Fire/explosion risk:** carbonaceous and metal dusts can burn or explode with air and an ignition source; self-heating possible.

## 3. PPE
- **Double nitrile gloves** (remove outer gloves in a hood and bag them)
- Lab coat with sleeves to the wrist; safety glasses or goggles
- Respirator with N/R/P-100 (HEPA) filters if working outside ventilation with materials that could become airborne

## 4. Controlling Hazards
- Handle in non-airborne forms (solution, substrate) when possible; otherwise HEPA-filtered local capture or glove box; else a fume hood.
- Vent tube furnaces/reactors to a hood; never exhaust nanoparticle aerosols indoors.
- No eating/drinking except in Clean Areas; sharps precautions.
- Clean spills promptly; **wet wipe** surfaces daily or use bench paper; wet wipe and HEPA-vacuum equipment before reuse or disposal; sticky walk-off mat at the exit.

## 5. Emergency Preparedness
Know eyewash/shower, Emergency Guide, first aid kit, printed SDSs, correct extinguisher (Fire & Life Safety (858) 534-7525). Spills: only very small, trained; PPE; respiratory protection if airborne; **do not brush or sweep** dry nanoparticles; dispose of materials as hazardous waste.

## 6. Exposure Response
Treat every exposure seriously. Ambulance: UCPD (858) 534-4357; Poison Control (800) 222-1222. Skin/eyes: flush at least 15 minutes. Ingestion: immediate medical attention.

## 7. Waste
Treat all engineered nanoparticle waste as hazardous unless known otherwise; alert EH&S technicians when requesting collection (hazwaste@ucsd.edu, (858) 534-2753).

# Preventing Reproductive Hazards in the Workplace

Exposure to certain chemicals, radioisotopes, and biological agents may cause infertility, miscarriage, and birth defects.

## Requirements for Researchers
Understand material characteristics; know risks and exposure pathways; review and acknowledge lab-specific mitigation and protocols; complete training before work; follow safety plans and SOPs. Chemical reproductive hazards must be assigned to the **Reproductive Hazard HCP**; infectious reproductive hazards have agent-specific HCPs. The PI must approve each HCP before work.

## Declaring Reproductive Planning (Optional, Confidential)
1. Contact Occupational Health (COEM-campus@health.ucsd.edu). 2. Complete the Reproductive Hazard Self-Assessment. 3. Discuss with EH&S Research Safety. 4. Follow-up Occupational Health consultation if needed. Radiation workers also follow the declared pregnancy process.

## Assessing the Hazards
Use risk assessment resources and the Proposition 65 list; review SDSs and HCPs; complete a Laboratory Hazard Assessment (LHAT).

## Controlling the Hazards
Eliminate/substitute; seek medical consultation; follow storage guidelines; use fume hoods, BSCs, and engineering controls; work only in designated areas; minimize quantities; use plastic-backed absorbent bench paper; wash hands; wear LHAT-specified PPE; dispose of waste properly.

## PI/Supervisor Responsibilities
Explain health effects and exposure routes; train on SOPs, engineering controls, and PPE; document lab-specific procedures; update documentation; inform workers of changes; keep training records.

## Students
Consult Student Health Services or a primary care provider; arrange accommodations with the professor, PI, or department.

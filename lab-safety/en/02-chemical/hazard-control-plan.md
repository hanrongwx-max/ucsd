# Hazard Control Plan

## Overview

The Hazard Control Plan (HCP) Application provides HCPs for researchers working with hazardous chemicals, biological materials, equipment, or processes. At UC San Diego, **an HCP is a standard operating procedure** that includes risk assessments, planning, and step-by-step safety protocols. It is on the My Research Safety portal, operated by EH&S.

## Requirements

- Investigators must implement HCPs as SOPs. Researchers must review, acknowledge, and adopt the HCPs assigned by their investigator.
- PIs use the application to determine and adopt required HCPs, provide detailed safe-work instructions, assign lab personnel to each HCP, and keep a repository of HCPs.

## Maintaining Your Personnel List

My Profiles → Lab Personnel → add missing members via People Search → assign an HCP to each employee.

## HCP Template Categories

1. **Chemical HCPs** — approved by the CSSC; individual or grouped by hazard class. Labs using any "High Hazard" chemical must use the chemical-specific HCP.
2. **Biological Materials HCPs** — approved by the IBC; assigned when materials are added to the BUA. Materials must also appear on an approved BUA and/or IACUC protocol.
3. **Hazardous Equipment and Processes HCPs** — e.g., autoclaves, microtomes, animal perfusion.

**Core (uneditable) sections:** header and general information; Required Training/Approvals; Administrative Controls; Engineering Controls; PPE; Handling/Storage/Purchasing; Spill Response; Exposure Response; Hazardous Waste Disposal; References and Resources. Research staff must not modify or weaken them.

**Editable sections** (both required before PI approval):
- **Summary:** why/how the material, equipment, or process is used.
- **Lab Specific Instructions:** quantities, concentrations, conditions of use, unusual hazards, genetic modifications, aerosol-generating procedures, PI restrictions, location, scale-up restrictions, physical conditions (heating, pressure, cooling).

## Determining Which HCPs You Need — Chemical Evaluator

Chemical Evaluator → Inventory HCP Requirements → select PI. Results show chemical name, CAS RN, required HCP, and action (Create HCP / View HCP / Add to HCP).

**Creating an HCP:** My HCP → Create HCP → choose PI and template → Create; or HCP Templates → select → Create Lab HCP. Saved as a draft.

**Important:** The PI must approve HCP assignments, and personnel must review the plan and acknowledge their assignment email **before beginning work**. All personnel must be on the PI's lab roster and an approved Laboratory Hazard Assessment Tool (LHAT).

**Tagging out:** attach an "HCP Required" sticker (PDF: https://blink.ucsd.edu/_files/safety-tab/research/5160-labels-hcp-required.pdf) to keep unused chemicals; they may not be used until an HCP exists, staff are assigned and signed off, and the PI approves.

## Completing Your HCP

1. **Step 1 — Project Summary / Lab Specific Instructions** (save each; unsaved work is lost).
2. **Step 2 — HCP Laboratory Personnel** (roster must be current).
3. **Step 3 — Current Chemical List** (not required for BIO HCPs).
4. **Approval** — the PI selects "Approve Hazard Control Plan" (or a lab contact sends a notification to the PI).

## Assignment and Confirmation

Personnel receive a "Hazard Control Plan Acknowledgement Request" email, click to confirm, log in, and confirm they read the HCP; the PI and lab contact receive confirmation.

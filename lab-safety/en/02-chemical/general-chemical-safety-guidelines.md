# General Chemical Safety Guidelines

These guidelines apply to all UC San Diego laboratories, shops, and studios and meet the requirements of the Chemical Hygiene Plan. Practice basic safety habits to reduce risk, and pay attention to both the health and physical hazards of every material you use. "Never work alone when hazardous chemicals are involved."

## 1. Maintain an Organized and Orderly Facility

**Work area**
- Keep the work area clean and uncluttered.
- Do not play pranks or engage in horseplay.
- Never leave these unattended: ongoing chemical reactions; exposed sharps (needles, razor blades, etc.); energized electrical, mechanical, or heating equipment.

**Chemical storage and inventory**
- Follow the chemical storage and compatibility guidelines.
- Keep chemical inventories lean and well managed to avoid fire code violations.

**Corridors**
- Keep corridors free of hazardous materials at all times (see Corridor Safety Requirements). Certain non-hazardous materials may be stored in exit corridors only if seismically secured and pre-approved by EH&S.
- Contacts: EH&S Chemical Hygiene Officer ehschem@ucsd.edu; labs — Research Assistance Program; shops/studios — EH&S General Safety, gensafety@ucsd.edu, (858) 534-7513.

## 2. Communicate Hazards to Everyone in the Facility

- Post: warning signs near dangerous equipment, reactions, or conditions; a chemical abbreviation list near the lab entrance (PDF: https://blink.ucsd.edu/_files/safety-tab/research/chemical/abbrev.pdf); PPE requirements for entry, if applicable.
- Label all containers.
- Keep containers closed except when in use, including hazardous waste containers.

## 3. Follow Basic Safety Procedures

**Evaluate the hazards**
- Read the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) before starting work with any chemical.
- Follow hazard control plans for extremely hazardous materials, with particular attention to high-hazard chemicals and chemical carcinogens.

**Never underestimate risk**
- Do not pipette by mouth.
- Never smell chemicals to identify them.
- Assume any mixture is more hazardous than its most toxic component, and that substances of unknown toxicity are highly toxic.

**Electrical safety**
- Keep electrical panels visible and unobstructed.
- Know how your circuits are labeled so equipment can be de-energized quickly in an emergency.
- Do not use extension cords as permanent wiring; unplug them at the end of the workday.
- Mount multi-plug adaptors a few inches off the floor to avoid water damage.
- Never connect multi-plug adaptors in series.
- Replace damaged or frayed cords immediately.

**Personal habits**
- Do not eat, drink, store food, smoke, or apply cosmetics where chemicals are in use, except in clearly marked Clean Areas.
- Wash hands frequently and before eating.
- Keep loose hair tied back.

## 4. Use Engineering Controls

Use chemical fume hoods and other engineering controls as needed.

**Vacuum trapping** — a trap between the experiment and the vacuum source protects the pump and piping, protects people who work on vacuum lines, and prevents vapors from being released back into the lab or exhaust. In a 2009 UC San Diego incident, an explosion from improper trapping destroyed a pump and left the vacuum system out of service for months.

- **Particulates:** filtration that captures the particle size range generated.
- **Aqueous or non-volatile liquids:** a room-temperature filter flask is usually adequate.
- **Solvents and other volatile liquids:** a cold trap large and cold enough to condense vapors, followed by a filter flask.
- **Highly reactive, corrosive, or toxic gases:** a sorbent canister or scrubbing device.
- **Cold traps:** dry ice slush (to −78 °C) with isopropanol or ethanol; avoid acetone.
- **Liquid nitrogen:** only with sealed or evacuated equipment, with extreme caution — if the system is opened while the bath contacts the trap, oxygen can condense and react violently with organic material.

See PPE requirements for research and non-research facilities.

## 6. Follow Requirements for High-Hazard Chemicals

- Create a hazard control plan (HCP) for work with high-hazard chemicals using the HCP application (Chemical Hazard Use Application, CHUA).
- Review the high-hazard chemicals list and the Specific Chemical Guidelines.
- At UC San Diego, an HCP is a standard operating procedure (SOP).

## 7. Prepare for Accidents and Emergencies

- Never work alone when hazardous chemicals are involved.
- **Spills:** clean up only very small quantities and only if properly trained; specially trained personnel handle all others. Keep a fully stocked chemical spill kit accessible and train personnel on when it is safe to use.
- **Know the location and use of:** telephones; Emergency Guide; first-aid kit; fire extinguishers and fire alarm pull stations (check extinguishers monthly); eyewash stations and emergency showers.

**Emergency override buttons (EOBs)** — provide maximum room ventilation during a chemical emergency.
- Activate only if you believe lab air has been affected. Open the clear cover and push PUSH HERE.
- Evacuate everyone immediately and stay out until the room is cleared.
- Call 9-1-1 from campus phones; UCSD Police and EH&S are notified automatically.
- Authorized personnel deactivate the EOB and declare the space safe.

**First-aid kits and Emergency Guides** are provided by EH&S; the Area Safety Coordinator monitors supplies. Contact ehsweb@ucsd.edu or (858) 534-3660 for replacements.

## 8. Dispose of Chemical Waste According to UCSD Guidelines

- Identify Hazardous Chemical Waste; Known Hazardous and Extremely Hazardous Wastes; Store and Dispose of Hazardous Chemical Waste; How to Use Hazardous Waste Tags.
- **Disposing of hazardous waste through sinks, intentional evaporation, or regular trash is prohibited.** Individuals may be held liable for violations.

# Chemical Compatibility Guidelines

Separate and store chemicals by compatibility group.

## Segregate Incompatible Chemicals

Segregating incompatible chemicals is the most important rule of chemical storage. It prevents accidental mixing that could cause fire, explosion, or toxic gases. Hazardous reactions in storage can result from: accidental breakage; container failure; fires and earthquakes; mixing of gases or vapors from poorly closed containers; incompatibles stored together because of mislabeling.

## Chemical Compatibility Groups

Store each group separately (separate cabinets or tubs/secondary containers) and label each container and location with its group:
flammable liquids; compressed gases; volatile poisons; acids; liquid bases; liquid oxidizers; non-volatile liquid poisons; metal hydrides and pyrophorics; dry solids.

### Flammable Liquids (flashpoint below 100 °F)
- Examples: all alcohols, acetone, acetaldehyde, acetonitrile, amyl acetate, benzene, cyclohexane, dimethyldichlorosilane, dioxane, diethyl ether, ethyl acetate, hexane, hydrazine, methyl butane, piperidine, pyridine, some scintillation liquids, all silanes, tetrahydrofuran, toluene, triethylamine, xylene.
- May be stored with volatile poisons **or** liquid bases, but not with both.

### Compressed Gases
- Examples: oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, arsine, acetylene.
- Store securely mounted. Segregate oxygen from flammable gases. Store acutely toxic and toxic gases in gas cabinets or fume hoods.

### Volatile Poisons
- Examples: carbon tetrachloride, chloroform, dimethylformamide, dimethyl sulfate, formamide, formaldehyde, halothane, mercaptoethanol, methylene chloride, phenol.
- Store in a ventilated cabinet. May be stored with flammable liquids only if no bases are present.

### Acids
- **Important:** segregate acids from chemicals that generate toxic or flammable gases on contact (cyanide salts, metal sulfides, calcium carbide) and from reactive metals (sodium, potassium, magnesium).
- Store in a ventilated corrosive cabinet if possible, inside a non-corrosive secondary container. Avoid contact with bases.
- **Oxidizing mineral acids** (sulfuric, nitric, chromic, perchloric): store separately from organic acids; double contain. Perchloric acid must be isolated from acetic anhydride, bismuth, alcohol, paper, wood, oil, ether, grease, sulfuric acid, and especially acetic acid.
- **Non-oxidizing mineral acids** (hydrochloric, hydrofluoric, phosphoric, hydroiodic): hydrofluoric acid (HF) is a high-hazard chemical requiring an approved HCP.
- **Organic acids** (acetic, butyric, formic, propionic): store separately from oxidizing mineral acids; may be stored with organic solvents unless the SDS says otherwise.

### Liquid Bases
- Examples: sodium hydroxide, ammonium hydroxide, calcium hydroxide, glutaraldehyde.
- Store in tubs or trays in a normal cabinet; avoid acids; may be stored with flammables if volatile poisons are not present.

### Liquid Oxidizers
- Examples: ammonium persulfate, hydrogen peroxide.
- Store in a ventilated corrosive cabinet; double contain — they react with nearly everything and may cause explosions.

### Non-Volatile Liquid Poisons
- Examples: acrylamide solutions, Coomassie blue, diethylpyrocarbonate, diisopropyl fluorophosphate, uncured epoxy resins, ethidium bromide, triethanolamine. (Includes carcinogens and highly toxic chemicals.)
- Store in a normal cabinet away from other materials; may be stored with non-hazardous liquids (buffers, salt solutions); double contain quantities over one liter.

### Metal Hydrides and Pyrophorics (air or water reactive)
- Examples: sodium borohydride, calcium hydride, lithium aluminum hydride. Most react violently with water.
- Ensure a Type D fire extinguisher is available. Store in a waterproof double container; may be stored with dry solids.

### Dry Solids
- Examples: cyanogen bromide, ethylmaleimide, oxalic acid, potassium cyanide, sodium cyanide.
- Store dry solids above liquids. **Keep liquid poisons below cyanide- or sulfide-containing solids** — a spill onto these would release poisonous gas.
- Picric acid may be stored with dry solids but must be checked regularly for dryness; **when completely dry, picric acid is explosive** and may detonate from shock or friction.

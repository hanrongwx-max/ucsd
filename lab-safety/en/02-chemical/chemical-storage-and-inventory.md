# Chemical Storage and Inventory

How UC San Diego personnel should store, label, and track chemicals. Departments are responsible for safely and securely managing chemical supplies and complying with fire code allowances. The procedures are intended to meet the California Fire Code (CFC) and the Community Right-to-Know Act.

## Segregate Incompatible Chemicals

- Store chemicals by compatibility group, **not alphabetically**.
- Keep each compatible group in its own cabinet, or separate it with tubs (e.g., polypropylene "Nalgene" tubs) or secondary containers.
- Label cabinets and tubs by compatibility group (e.g., "Mineral (Inorganic) Acids" for HCl, H2SO4, H3PO4).

## Basic Chemical Storage

- Keep containers capped and closed when not in use (County of San Diego regulations).
- Store hazardous materials on lower shelves, not overhead.
- Use seismic restraints so containers do not slide off open shelves during an earthquake.
- Protect drains from spills. Do not use sinks for chemical storage or secondary containment.
- Containers must be free of corrosion, damage, and visible contamination.

## Select Appropriate Containers

When transferring a chemical: use a sturdy, sealable, compatible container; ask ehschem@ucsd.edu about compatibility; label the new container.

## Label Chemical Containers

- **All transferred chemicals:** chemical name or abbreviation; concentration; hazard warning.
- **Degrading chemicals, peroxide formers, air- and water-reactives:** date received; date opened; date tested.
- **Abbreviations:** post a chemical abbreviation sheet in the lab.
- **Refrigerators:** "No Food Storage" label on refrigerators used for chemicals; "No Flammable Storage" label on refrigerators that are not approved flammable storage units.

## Chemical Inventories

Records should include: chemical name; CAS number; building, room, and sublocation; container size and unit of measure; number of containers; physical state. Time-sensitive chemicals also need date received, opened, and tested. EH&S verifies inventories during safety audits. PIs, lab contacts, and DSOs can view/export inventory in My Research Safety.

## Keep Inventories Lean

- Avoid duplicate orders; order only what is needed — **a six-month supply is a good target**.
- Use **ChemCycle** (over 6,000 chemicals, free) before ordering. To donate usable surplus: create a tag in the Online Waste Tag Program, select "Chemcycle" as content type, print the tag, request pickup, and place the container in your waste collection area.
- Stay within fire code limits.
- Dispose of unwanted chemicals promptly through the EH&S Hazardous Waste Program, at no charge.

## Fire Code Limits: Maximum Allowable Quantities (MAQs)

- **Occupancy:** Group B (most UC San Diego research and shop facilities) vs. Group H (high-hazard; a few areas).
- **Control areas:** most Group B research buildings have four control areas; limits apply by control area, not by room.
- **MAQ** depends on state (solid, liquid, gas), storage or use conditions, and mitigating circumstances.

Sample MAQs (demonstration only):

| Material | Sample MAQ |
|---|---|
| Flammable gas (storage or closed system) | 1,000 cubic feet |
| Flammable liquids (storage) | 120 gallons |
| Oxidizer gas (storage or closed system) | 1,500 cubic feet |
| Explosives (storage) | 1 pound |
| Pyrophoric gas (storage or closed system) | 50 cubic feet; not permitted in unsprinklered buildings |

**Mitigating circumstances:** fully sprinklered buildings may increase allowed quantities by 100%; approved storage cabinets, gas cabinets, or exhausted enclosures may increase by another 100%. In buildings that are not fully sprinklered, pyrophorics (e.g., alkyllithiums, alkylzincs, diborane, arsine, phosphine) may not be used in any quantity.

Assistance: researchers — your EH&S RAP specialist; non-research — EH&S General Safety, gensafety@ucsd.edu, (858) 534-7513.

## Regulated and High-Hazard Materials (special storage requirements)

Chemical carcinogens; compressed gas; controlled substances; flammable liquids; peroxide-forming materials; piranha solution; pyrophoric and other highly reactive materials; select agents; radioactive materials.

## Flammables and Pyrophorics

- Pyrophoric materials may be stored and used only in fully sprinklered buildings.
- Keep **Class D fire extinguishers** for flammable metals (Li, Na, K, Mg) and metal compounds (butyllithium, diethylzinc, lithium aluminum hydride).
- **Never store flammable liquids in standard or domestic refrigerators or freezers** — use special flammable storage units.

## Environmental Rooms

Never use cold or warm rooms to store flammable or hazardous materials (ignition sources, little outside air). Small quantities (up to **500 ml**) may be used there.

## Annual Hazardous Materials Inventory

EH&S conducts annual chemical inventory reviews as part of the County-required Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP). Report all chemicals in any quantity meeting GHS criteria (pyrophoric, water reactive, explosive, flammable, toxic, peroxide forming, corrosive, oxidizing, reducing, irritants/sensitizers, health hazards, any compressed gas). Unsafe or non-compliant inventories are reported to the department, CSSC, and the Campus and State Fire Marshals. HMBP reporting to CERS is handled by EH&S (ehsea@ucsd.edu).

**Notice:** Disposing of hazardous waste through sinks, intentional evaporation, or regular trash is illegal.

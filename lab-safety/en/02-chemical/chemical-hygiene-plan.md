# Chemical Hygiene Plan

Plan last reviewed: December 10, 2025.

## Overview

- UC San Diego's Chemical Hygiene Plan (CHP) sets out procedures, equipment, personal protective equipment (PPE), and work practices that protect personnel from health and physical hazards of laboratory chemical work.
- The CHP complies with Cal/OSHA's Laboratory Standard (CCR Title 8, §5191).
- The web-based CHP and Laboratory Safety Manual must be readily available wherever hazardous chemicals are present.
- Core rule: **never work alone when hazardous chemicals are involved.**

## Annual Review

The Chemical Hygiene Officer (CHO) reviews and evaluates the CHP at least once a year and updates it as needed.

## Hazardous Chemical Definition

Per CCR Title 8, §5191, a hazardous chemical is one with statistically significant evidence, from at least one scientifically sound study, of acute or chronic health effects in exposed employees. Health hazards include carcinogens, toxic and highly toxic agents, reproductive toxins, irritants, corrosives, sensitizers, hepatotoxins, nephrotoxins, neurotoxins, and agents affecting blood-forming systems or damaging the lungs, skin, eyes, or mucous membranes.

## Regulatory Background

- Federal OSHA Laboratory Standard (1990); Cal/OSHA Laboratory Standard CCR Title 8 §5191 (1991).
- Goal: protect workers from overexposure by informing them of chemical hazards and keeping exposures within OSHA Permissible Exposure Limits, through a written CHP.
- **Scope:** all labs that use, store, or handle potentially hazardous chemicals, and all personnel in those facilities. Not research involving only radiological or biological materials. Research with more than one hazard type must meet all applicable requirements.
- "Particularly hazardous substances" (select carcinogens, reproductive toxins, chemicals with high acute toxicity) need stricter procedures such as fume hoods, glove boxes, and designated work areas.

## Responsibilities

- **CSSC:** oversees chemical agent use.
- **PIs / lab supervisors:** implement the CHP, Laboratory Safety Manual, and the Administrative Responsibilities Handbook; use the online HCP application to document and train staff.
- **Director of EH&S:** cognizant administrator for health, safety, and environmental programs.
- **EH&S Chemical Hygiene Officer:** administers the institutional CHP (ehschem@ucsd.edu).
- **Individuals:** perform activities safely and follow current policies.

## Safety Training

Required during new employee orientation and before starting work with hazardous chemicals; and when job aspects change or new materials or procedures are introduced.

**New employees must complete two courses before working in a university laboratory:**
1. UC Laboratory Safety Fundamentals
2. Annual Laboratory Hazards Training

## Exposure Control Practices

- Perform a risk assessment **before** using any hazardous material. Limit exposure at the source.
- Follow the NIOSH hierarchy: elimination, substitution, modification, containment, engineering controls, work practices; **PPE as a last resort**.

**Administrative controls:** elimination; substitution with less dangerous chemicals; modification (dry run first); work practices (follow SOPs, never work alone); segregation (designated, restricted-access area).

**Engineering controls:**
- Building ventilation: at least **6 air changes per hour (ACH)** of outside air, airflow from lower- to higher-hazard areas.
- Chemical fume hoods: among the most reliable engineering controls when used properly.
- Biosafety cabinets: HEPA-filtered; required when working with biohazards.
- Other containment: glove boxes, gas cabinets.

**Work practices:** decontaminate areas and equipment after each use.

**PPE:** when hazards cannot be eliminated; check LHAT and HCP requirements.

## General Chemical Safety Guidelines

### Maintain an organized facility
- Keep work areas clean and uncluttered. No practical jokes or horseplay.
- Never leave ongoing reactions, exposed sharps, or energized electrical, mechanical, or heating equipment unattended.
- Follow storage and compatibility guidelines; keep inventories lean.
- Keep corridors free of hazardous materials at all times.

### Communicate hazards
- Post warning signs near dangerous equipment, reactions, or conditions.
- Post the chemical abbreviations list near the lab entrance, with PPE requirements for entry.
- Label all containers and keep them closed except when in use, including waste containers.

### Follow basic safety procedures
- Read the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) before starting work with a chemical.
- Follow HCPs, especially for high-hazard chemicals and carcinogens.
- Never underestimate risk: no mouth pipetting; never smell chemicals to identify them; assume mixtures are more hazardous than their most toxic component; treat unknown-toxicity substances as highly toxic.
- Electrical safety: keep panels unobstructed; no extension cords as permanent wiring; mount multi-plug adaptors off the floor; never chain multi-plug adaptors; replace damaged cords.
- No eating, drinking, food storage, smoking, or cosmetics where chemicals are used, except in marked Clean Areas. Wash hands frequently.
- Tie back loose hair.

### Vacuum trapping
- Place a trap between apparatus and vacuum source. A 2009 UC San Diego explosion from improper trapping destroyed the building vacuum pump.
- Particulates: suitable filtration. Aqueous/non-volatile liquids: room-temperature filter flask. Solvents/volatiles: adequate cold trap followed by filter flask. Highly reactive, corrosive, or toxic gases: sorbent canister/scrubber and a dedicated lab pump — not the building vacuum.
- **Replace filters at least every 90 days** or sooner; dispose of used filters as hazardous waste.
- Cold traps: dry ice with isopropanol or ethanol (to −78 °C); avoid acetone.
- Liquid nitrogen traps: only with sealed or evacuated equipment — opening the system can condense oxygen that may react violently with organics.

### Prepare for accidents and emergencies
- Never work alone with hazardous chemicals.
- Only trained personnel may clean up very small spills; others need specially trained responders. Keep a stocked spill kit.
- Know locations of phones, Emergency Guide, first-aid kits, fire extinguishers, alarm pull stations, eyewash/showers. Check extinguishers monthly.
- **Emergency override buttons (EOBs):** activate only if lab air has been impacted; evacuate immediately; call 9-1-1; authorized personnel reset and clear the space.
- First-aid kits are provided by EH&S (ehsweb@ucsd.edu for replacements).

## SOP / Hazard Control Plan (HCP)

- SOPs are required for hazardous chemical work; at UC San Diego an HCP is the SOP.
- CSSC-approved HCPs are required for **high-hazard chemicals**; EH&S-reviewed HCPs cover other hazard classes.
- An HCP is complete only when: Summary and Lab-Specific Instructions are filled out; all relevant personnel are assigned; **the PI submits (approves) it before work begins** and whenever hazards change; personnel confirm assignment; it is kept current.
- **Particularly hazardous substances** require additional protection: designated area; containment devices; safe removal of contaminated waste; decontamination procedures.
- Unused chemicals can be "tagged out" with an "SOP Required" sticker and may not be used until an HCP exists, staff are assigned and sign off, and the PI approves.

## Prior Approval

PI approval is required before significant changes in chemical amounts, new equipment, new chemicals, new procedures, or new workspaces — watch especially for scale-up, unattended operations, and particularly hazardous substances.

## GHS, SDS, Inventory

- GHS: UN-adopted (2003) classification and labeling system with harmonized signal words, pictograms, and hazard/precaution statements.
- SDS: manufacturer document describing properties, toxicity, flash point, spill procedures, storage, exposure controls.
- Labs must maintain an inventory of hazardous substances in My Research Safety.

## Medical Consultation and Exams

Available when symptoms may relate to chemical use, when monitoring shows exposure above an action level/PEL, or after an event likely to cause hazardous exposure. Exams are supervised by a licensed physician, at no cost, without loss of pay.

## Reporting

- Report work-related injury or illness immediately.
- Chemistry/Biochemistry: call 9-1-1 for serious incidents; secure the scene and preserve evidence for at least 24 hours.

## Resources

- Flash chromatography and solvent purification systems each require a PI-approved HCP before any work.
- Piranha solution requires specific waste containers.
